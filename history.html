The retreat industry over time, 2015–2026

Reconstructed from the Wayback Machine: archived directory listings and the centres' own archived websites

Read this first Counts are lower bounds: Wayback captures only a sample of each directory each year. Exits confirmed by a centre's own site going dark are reliable; exits where a listing merely disappears are not — Retreat.Guru's archive collapses after 2020, so listing-only "exits" in 2015–16 and 2019–20 are largely frame artifacts. Centres delisted from a directory but still online are counted as alive. Price and staffing series are shown only once the full panel completes.

Census

How many centres we can prove existed each year — from directory listings and site snapshots — plus entries and exits. Treat the level as a floor; the robust finding is the growth. Closures are mostly unobservable: Retreat.Guru never links to a centre's own site, so when its listings vanish we cannot tell closure from delisting — only a handful of exits are site-confirmed.

Survival

Kaplan–Meier survival from a centre's first appearance: the share still operating after t years. Centres alive today are censored. Because most exits are listing-only (and partly frame artifacts), this curve is a lower bound on survival — true survival is likely higher.

From the longitudinal panel: the same centres' archived pages at successive years, run through the identical extraction. Only within-centre, duration-matched, per-night comparisons are shown — raw medians mix who happens to be captured each year.
